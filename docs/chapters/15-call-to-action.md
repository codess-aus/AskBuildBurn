# The Call to Action

You've read fifteen chapters. You've thought about your relationship with Copilot, your ownership of your code, your habits, your capacity, your team.

Now what?

---

## Not a To-Do List

This is not the chapter where you get a comprehensive to-do list. To-do lists at the end of books are where good intentions go to die.

Instead, this is an invitation. To make one choice. Then another. Then another.

The developers who change their practice don't do it all at once. They pick up one habit, see what it does, and build from there. Over weeks, over months, something different emerges. Something more deliberate. More sustainable. More genuinely theirs.

---

## The Three Commitments

If you close this book with three things, let them be these:

**1. Own your code.**

Every line. Every edge case. Every function you didn't type but you approved. This isn't burden — it's power. When you own the code, you know it. When you know it, you can improve it, defend it, and build on it.

**2. Ask more.**

Not just of Copilot. Of yourself. Of your team. Ask what this does. Ask why it works this way. Ask what could go wrong. Ask how we'd catch it. Asking is not weakness — it's the practice that keeps you sharp and the code safe.

**3. Protect your capacity.**

You are not infinitely renewable. The quality of your code over time depends on the quality of your attention. Your attention depends on rest, on curiosity, on the kind of work that replenishes you. Protect those things, not because they feel good (though they do), but because they are how you keep doing good work.

---

## For Your Team

If you lead people, this book is also about them.

The culture you build — around asking, reviewing, owning, and recovering — is the culture they work in. If you celebrate speed over understanding, they'll sacrifice understanding. If you make asking safe, they'll ask.

You have more influence over the sustainability of your team's practice than any tool. Use it.

---

## The Ongoing Conversation

GitHub Copilot is going to keep changing. Agent Mode will become more capable. The things it can do autonomously will expand. The review burden will increase proportionally — unless you build the habits now that scale with it.

This book is a snapshot of where we are. The principles — ownership, asking, reviewing, protecting capacity — will apply when the tools look completely different.

Build on principles. Update your playbook. Keep asking.

---

    The goal was never to move fast. The goal was to build things you're proud of, in a way you can sustain, with tools that help rather than hollow you out.
