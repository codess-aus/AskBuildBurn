## Why I Wrote This

I've been a developer long enough to remember when "productivity tool" meant a better terminal. I've watched the tools change dramatically — and watched the pressure on developers change with them.

When GitHub Copilot arrived, I was excited. Genuinely. The capability was remarkable and still is. But I also started noticing a pattern in the communities I was part of: developers using AI assistance were moving faster, but some of them were burning out faster too.

The connection wasn't the tool. It was the habits.

When AI assistance accelerates your work without accelerating your understanding, you get more output and less ownership. That gap — between what you produce and what you truly know — is where anxiety lives. And sustained anxiety, unchecked, becomes burnout.

I wrote this book because I wanted to name that pattern. And because I believe the solution isn't to slow down or step back from AI tools — it's to build the habits that keep you genuinely in the driver's seat.

---

## What I Believe About Software Development

**Ownership matters.** The developer who understands their code — even the parts they didn't write — is more effective, more resilient, and more valuable than the one who ships fast and hopes.

**Asking is a skill.** The best engineers I know ask the most questions. Not because they know the least — because they know that asking is how you know more.

**Sustainability is not optional.** A developer who burns out in two years is worse for their team, their code, and themselves than one who's still curious and effective in ten. Sustainable practice is not a soft concern. It's an engineering concern.

**AI assistance is a force multiplier — for better or worse.** It amplifies your habits. Good habits get better. Bad habits get dangerous. The work is to build the right habits.

---

## The Community I'm Building This For

This book is for developers who care. Who want to do good work, not just fast work. Who are feeling the friction of AI-assisted development and want a framework for navigating it.

It's for the developer who accepted a Copilot suggestion they didn't fully understand and shipped it anyway, and is still thinking about it.

It's for the team lead who wants to set their team up to thrive with AI tools, not just adopt them.

It's for the person who loves this craft and wants to keep loving it.
